## Ok, got it the way i want it

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · May 8, 2007|40_

> I wanted to repost these direction from Brian as to how to hook up an M470. They are below:
>
> Tommy,
>
> Do you have the manual and schematic for the M470? I don't have the M470 manual, but the PCB's for the M470 & M470A are the same, so you can download the M470A manual and use the schematic - just ignore the Aux 3 & 4 and pan circuits.
>
> You've got several options here, depending on how you want to ultimately use the module. First off, you understand that P1 W19 is 2 pins, right? P1-W and P1-19.
>
> Mic 1 to P1-W(+) & P1-19 (-), shield to P1-Y or P1-21
>
> Line in to P2-A (+) & P2-1 (-), shield to P2-D or P2-4
>
> There is an insert for EQ at P1-22 (send) and P1-Z (return). If you want to retain this option you can wire this to a 1/4" insert jack (has breaking contacts that open the circuit when a plug is inserted).
> Send: P1-22 (+) and P1-21 or P1-Y (Gnd)
> Return: P1-Z (+) and P1-21 or P1-Y (Gnd)
> If you don't want to retain this option, you'll need to jumper P1-22 to P1-Z, or you'll get no output.
>
> There is another insert after the pre-amp section.
> If you do want to use this insert, it will work similar to the EQ insert above.
> Send: P2-8 (+) and P2-J (-), no shield conn.
> Return: P2-6 (+) and P2-F (-), Shield to P2-D or P2-4.
> Again, you'll need to use a insert jack (6-pin) that makes these contacts when no plug is inserted, or you'll get no output.
> If you don't want to use this insert you'll need to jumper P2-8 to P2-6 and P2-J to P2-F, or else you'll get no output.
>
> Next is the Attenuator circuit, where the fader would normally go. If you're going to use an attenuator (pot or fader), which might be useful, you'll want to connect it thus:
> Send: P2-7 (+) and P2-H (-), No Shield conn.
> Return: P2-5 (+) and P2-E (-), Shield to P2-D or P2-4.
> To bypass the attenuator, you can jumper P2-7 to P2-5 and P2-H to P2-E.
>
> The direct out comes after the attenuator and before the post-fader aux sends.
> You can use the Direct out from P2-C (+) & P2-3 (-), shield to P2-D or P2-4. This is also where you would connect a VU meter, if you wanted one.
>
> Wiring it like this, the M1 and M2 buttons will be ineffective, and both aux send circuits will still be available if you choose to use them.
> Aux1 send: P1-12 (+) and P1-N (-)
> Aux2 send: P1-11 (+) and P1-M (-)
>
> Or, you could skip all the post-fader circuitry all together and take your direct out from P2-7 (+) and P2-H (-). This is actuall where the Pre-fader aux sends come from.
>
> Or, if you don't care about the channel on/off button, you could even take your direct out from P2-8 (+) and P2-J (-), and forget about that second insert section, too. The PFL is actually taken right after that second insert section, so this would essentially be the PFL signal.
>
> For power, I assume you'll be using the 271-2009-ND power supply from digikey. You'll want to use the +/- 24V connections - there's three output connections from the power supply: +24, common, and -24, which should be connected thus:
> +24 VDC to P2-21
> 24 VDC Common to P2-20
> -24 VDC to P2-19
> Chassis Ground to P2-22
>
> The 24V connection at P2-Y (+) and P2-Z (-) is only to power the lamp in the channel on/off button, which, by the way, does not cut power from the module but shunts the output of the pre-amp section and disconnects the signal from the module's output section. PFL still works with the switch in "off". Since you're using the +/- 24VDC power supply, you can jumper P2-21 to P2-Y and P2-20 to P2-Z to make this lamp light up.
>
> Hopefully that covers it all.
>
> Brian

## Ward Beck pre's

_WBS INFO · May 26, 2007|40_

> Ward Beck had a design that worked and put the same thing in an entire series so the pre-amp is almost identical in all of the L series it's how the signal goes out of the module that is different. It really depends on here you tap the output. 
>
> The M470's are the most flexible in terms of routing, so if you're looking for a console with flexibility fit them with M470's. within the M470's their are several variations different aux buses, pan pots master outputs. The big one and most flexible is the M470C it has 4 Aux buses, 6 sub group sends and pan pot. since some consoles were used as mono only, some M470's dont even have a pan pot. All M470's are swappable, so if you have a console with them any variation will pop in without any problems. I keep a set of M470C's kicking around so when a M470A module in my console dies or needs service they are standing by. 
>
> The M490's are identical pre-amps as th M470's just smaller with a fader fitted in to the module. they were used in radio broadcast consoles. Limited routing and they have a very flexible remote button section. 
> The M480 is a great module it combines a bit of the L series pre-amp and the EQ of say a scaled back M472 (the M480 has fixed LF and HF points) the M480's were only fitted in the T1202 transportable mixer. 
>
> The M460's use a different transformer and a different booster amp setup various eq and routing options are availible on these modules, they make great rack mounted pieces
> The M461 is identical input section as M460's but has no EQ
> The M441's are the most sought after and the closest to the original neve design that was copied. Best transformers, detended pots very little circuit boards all hand made. great pieces! 
> The M600 is the same vintage as the M441's they were designed to be remote pre-amps or used in intercom racks. they share some components as the M441's but are a rack tray module with only a gain pot not a console module
>
> i hope this helps, and if I'm wrong on some details sorry in advance

## Pan pot as gain??

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Feb 7, 2008|41_

> Hi Guys, you can use the pan pot on the M470 as a QUAZI level control but you must bypass resistors R120 and R121 if you want to use output M2. Make sure switch M2 is cleaned and the pan engage on the M470a. The output is taken from pins A & 1 on P2. Also bypass (strap) Z and 22 together on P1. On P2 strap E to H, F to J, 5 to 7 and 6 to 8. You can also take a direct out at C & 3 on P2.
>
> R120 and R121 are summing buss isolation resistors and will become attenuators when connected direct into a 10K or lower input impedance. On M470 modules without the pan control you can use Aux 1 pins N & 10 on P1. Bypass (strap) R124 and R125 and clean the Pre/Post Aux 1 switch plus the Aux 1 select switch. The Aux pot will be the output control. There will be 15db difference between Pre and Post so this output can work nicely with -10db systems or +4db systems.
>
> Cheers, Dave Thomas www.aamicrophones.com

— **kuzub**, Feb 13, 2010|43

> Changing R124 and 125 to 75ohms will change the balanced output impedance from 34.8kOhm to 4.8kOhm... A big improvement, but still reasonably high.
>
> Lets say you also change R92 and 93 to 75 as well. Your balanced output impedance is now 300Ohms... Great! I'm more than happy with that!
>
> (For simplicity here; I'm going to pretend that the Aux pot is Linear: just to make the math in my example easy to understand for the common forum-lurkers that read this in the future...:)
>
> Lets turn the Aux knob down to 10%... We're now putting a 500Ohm load between R92 and 93, which the output amplifier will see as 500+75+75, or 650 ohms.
>
> So right away, with nothing connected to the output, we're making that amplifier work very hard... Adding more gear to the output will only make it work harder.
> Harder working amps = more current used = more heat.
> Why push the physical performance of the amplifier to it's maximum, when its not needed?
>
> Lets put a +20dB sine wave through the amplifier, and have the Aux dial at 10%...
> +20dB is approximately 7.75V...
>
> The output amplifier is pumping 11.9mA through R92 and 93, and the aux pot... (7.75v/650Ohms)
> 11.9mA through the 500 Ohm resistance of the Aux pot works out to about 71milli-Watts of power-dissipation... all in the form of heat! ((500*11.9mA)*11.9mA) or (500*11.9mA^2)
>
> That doesn't sound like a lot... and it's not. But lets assume that the potentiometers are rated for 1/4 of a Watt... or 250mW.
> That rating is for the entire pot but we're only using 10% of the pot... so our little resistance is only rated for 25 mW... we're giving it 71!
> 10% of the potentiometer just melted, leaving the other 90% useless.... with out even connecting the PreAmp to anything!
>

> The resistors that Tony mentioned, and the Cap, are located right behind the Sensitivity / Gain potentiometer.
>
> From what I read:
> You have measured 48V on one side of the 6.8K resistors... (Not the side that goes to the microphone input)
>
> This tells me that your 48-Volt Switching method works, and you wired things correctly.
>
> But you are getting only 12-volts on the microphone connector, with or without a microphone, and only with the Pad on.
>
> What is causing the 48-volts to drop to 12... can be looked at as a Voltage Divider.
> Basically... you are limiting the 48-volt current with the 6.8K resistors, but something is trying to suck more current, causing the voltage to go down.
>
> If you take a good working Phantom system, and connect a large, power-hungry microphone and turn it on... and if you measure the phantom power on the microphone then... you will see a bit less than 48 Volts. Everything works fine, just physics causes that number to go down.
>
> But, because your voltage is going so far down to 12 volts, and because it does that with no microphone attached, and only when the pad is on.... I suspect that something inside the module is 'Out of Spec.'
>
> I believe, that when you turn the Pad on, it's now acting as a 'Power-Hungry Load' and sucking juice from the 6.8K resistors.
>
> The resistors in the Pad may be messed up... rare but it can happen. The pad might still appear to work by dropping the mic level, but it does not play well with the 48volts.
>
> My first guess, would be C2. A 1uF capacitor, most likely a Tant cap... it looks like a gum-drop.
>
> I've run into many of these small capacitors dying in WB modules... when they die, they usually become a short circuit.
>
> Find that cap, set your multi-meter to Continuity, or Beep mode... and see if you get a constant beep across the capacitor. (There could be a instant 'bleep' when you first test it, but it should not beep for ever.)
>
> You can also just remove the capacitor, turn on the modules, and try to apply phantom power without it... Just to test the idea.
>
> If you now get 48V with the pad in, you found the bad part! Replace it with any 1uF capacitor... This one does not need to be a special gum-drop one.
>
> Let us know. =)
>
> And...... That's my morning rant... now back to my coffee. =)
